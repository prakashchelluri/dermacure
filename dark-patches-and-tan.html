Dark patches, tan and uneven skin tone.

Sun makes most dark patches worse, and many can be controlled with sunscreen, the right creams and patience.

Book for this

What it is

In plain words.

Dark patches appear when the skin makes extra colour (melanin) in some areas. This includes tan, melasma, which shows as brown patches on the face, and the dark marks left after pimples, rashes or injuries. Most are harmless to health, but they are slow to fade.

You might notice

  • Brown or grey-brown patches on the cheeks, forehead, nose or upper lip, often the same on both sides.
  • Darker skin on the face, neck, arms and feet, where the sun reaches.
  • Dark marks that stay after a pimple, rash, burn or insect bite has healed.
  • Patches that get darker in summer or after time outdoors.

Why it happens.

01

Sun exposure

Sunlight causes tan, is the main trigger for melasma, and makes other dark patches darker. Even short daily exposure, and light coming through windows, can keep patches dark and bring them back after treatment.

02

Hormones and family tendency

Melasma is more common in women and often starts or gets worse in pregnancy or with hormonal pills. It can also run in families.

03

Irritation and steroid creams

Pimples, rashes, harsh scrubs, bleach and home remedies can leave dark marks as the skin heals. Fairness creams with steroids give a quick glow at first, then with longer use can cause thin skin, redness, pimples and patchy skin colour.

At the clinic

What gets checked first.

The dermatologist first looks at where the patches are, their pattern, and how deep the colour sits, sometimes with a special light. You will be asked about sun exposure, pregnancy, pills, and every cream or home remedy you have applied. If you have used a fairness or steroid cream, say so, because the skin may need to recover from it first.

What treatment can involve

Prescription medicines and creams

What it isDaily sunscreen along with creams that slow colour formation, used as prescribed, is the base of treatment for most dark patches.

What to expectPatches often start to fade in 2 to 3 months, deeper ones take longer, and the creams need a doctor's review because some cannot be used for long periods.

Chemical peels

What it isA peeling liquid removes the top layer of skin in a controlled way to help fade tan and surface patches.

What to expectPeels are done over several sittings and work only along with sunscreen, and a peel that is too strong can itself leave dark marks, so it must be chosen for your skin.

Laser toning

What it isA low-energy laser is passed over the skin to break up extra colour gradually.

What to expectIt needs multiple sittings, results differ from person to person, melasma can return afterwards, and in some people it leaves small light spots or darker patches, so it is used along with creams and sunscreen and not in place of them.

Q-switched laser (tattoos and pigment)

What it isA laser that targets pigment, used for certain well-defined dark spots and some deeper pigment such as certain birthmarks.

What to expectIt suits only some types of pigment, needs more than one sitting, and is not a first choice for melasma because it can leave the patch darker or lighter in some people.

Medical facials including HydraFacial

What it isA clinic facial that cleans the skin, removes dead cells and adds moisture.

What to expectIt can make dull skin look fresher for a short time, but it does not treat melasma or deeper patches.

Straight talk

Tan fades over weeks to a few months once the skin is protected from the sun. Melasma and deeper patches take several months or longer to fade, some do not clear fully, and sun can bring them back, so sunscreen continues even after they improve. Treatment works on the patches and does not change your natural skin colour.

What helps at home

  • Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning. Apply it again about every 2 hours if you are outdoors, and after sweating.
  • Use an umbrella, cap or scarf in strong sun.
  • Do not use fairness creams, bleach, lemon, or mixed creams from a shop or parlour.
  • Do not scrub or rub the patches. Rubbing can make them darker.

When to come in soon

See a dermatologist if a patch is spreading, if your skin has become thin, red or patchy after using a fairness cream, or if a single dark spot changes in size, shape or colour. Do not keep trying shop creams for months.

Questions people ask.

Will my melasma go away fully?

Melasma can be controlled, and in many people the patches fade a lot with treatment. It tends to return with sun exposure or hormonal changes, so sunscreen and follow-up are long term. No treatment can promise it will not come back.

Do I need sunscreen if I stay indoors or it is cloudy?

Yes. The light that darkens patches can pass through clouds and window glass. Daily sunscreen is part of the treatment, and other creams do not work well without it.

Will the treatment make me fairer?

No. Treatment aims to fade tan and dark patches so your skin tone looks even. It does not change the natural colour you were born with.

I have been using a fairness cream for months. Is it harmful?

It can be, because many such creams contain steroids. With long use these can thin the skin and cause redness, pimples and patchy skin colour. Bring the tube with you, and let the dermatologist guide you on stopping it, as the skin can flare when it is stopped.
