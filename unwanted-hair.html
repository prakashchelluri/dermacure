Unwanted hair and laser hair removal.

Laser can reduce unwanted hair over several sittings, and it helps to find the cause first.

Book for this

What it is

In plain words.

Some facial and body hair is normal, and how much you have depends on family and hormones. If the hair is thick, dark and bothers you, laser can reduce it. Laser gives hair reduction over several sittings. It is not one-time removal.

You might notice

  • Thick, dark hair on the chin, upper lip, jawline, chest or stomach in women.
  • Hair that grows back fast after threading, waxing or shaving.
  • Ingrown hairs, bumps or dark marks after repeated waxing or shaving.
  • New hair growth along with irregular periods, pimples or weight gain.

Why it happens.

01

Family and genes

Many people have more hair simply because it runs in the family. This is not a disease.

02

Hormonal causes

PCOS is the most common hormonal reason for thick facial hair in women, often with irregular periods and pimples. Other hormone problems are rare, but they need to be ruled out when hair growth is sudden.

03

Certain medicines

Some medicines can increase hair growth, including certain steroids and hormone tablets. Tell the doctor everything you take.

At the clinic

What gets checked first.

The doctor looks at where the hair is, how thick and dark it is, and your skin type. You will be asked about periods, weight changes, medicines and family history. Blood tests or a scan for PCOS may be advised before starting laser.

What treatment can involve

Laser hair removal

What it isLaser light is absorbed by the dark pigment in the hair and heats the root, which weakens it.

What to expectOnly hairs in the growing phase respond each time, so several sittings spaced weeks apart are needed, and some fine or light hair will remain.

Prescription medicines and creams

What it isWhen a hormone problem such as PCOS is the cause, medicines or a cream that slows facial hair growth may be given as prescribed.

What to expectMedicines take about six months to show a change, and hair growth can return when they are stopped.

Chemical peels

What it isA mild peel may be advised for dark marks left by ingrown hairs or repeated waxing.

What to expectMarks can fade slowly over several sittings, and a peel does nothing for the hair itself.

Straight talk

Sittings are usually spaced four to eight weeks apart, and many people need six or more to see a clear reduction. Laser does not remove every hair, and it does not work well on white, grey or very fine hair. Some hair can grow back over time, more so with PCOS, so maintenance sittings may be needed.

What helps at home

  • Shave the area before a sitting as advised. Do not wax, thread or pluck before or between sittings, because laser needs the root in place.
  • Stay out of strong sun and use sunscreen on treated areas. Tanned skin has a higher risk of burns and marks.
  • Mild redness and small bumps are common for a day or two. Cool the area and avoid hot water and scrubbing.
  • Tell the doctor about any new medicine or cream before each sitting.

When to come in soon

See a dermatologist before any laser if hair growth is sudden or fast, or comes with irregular periods, a deeper voice or thinning hair on the scalp. Come back early if you get blisters, burns or dark patches after a sitting, or if hair seems to grow more in or around the treated area.

Questions people ask.

Will the hair be gone for good after laser?

No. Laser can give long-lasting hair reduction, and the hair that returns is usually finer and lighter. Some people need a maintenance sitting now and then, more often if hormones are the cause.

Can laser be done on brown or dark skin?

Yes, when the right type of laser and settings are chosen for your skin. Darker skin has a higher risk of burns, dark patches or light patches, more so with wrong settings. Tell the doctor if you have a tan or have had marks after a procedure before.

Does laser hurt?

Most people feel a quick snap and heat, like a rubber band on the skin. Cooling is usually used to make it more comfortable. Sensitive areas such as the upper lip can sting more.

I have PCOS. Will laser still work?

Laser can reduce the hair, but hormones keep pushing new hair to grow. You may need more sittings and maintenance later. Treating the PCOS alongside usually gives a steadier result.
