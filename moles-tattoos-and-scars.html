Moles, skin tags, tattoos and scars.

Most moles and skin tags are harmless, but some need a check, and scars and tattoos can fade slowly with treatment.

Book for this

What it is

In plain words.

Moles are small spots, usually brown or black, and skin tags are soft bits of extra skin, often on the neck or underarms. Scars and stretch marks are marks left after injury, pimples, surgery or fast stretching of the skin. An unwanted tattoo is ink sitting deep in the skin, so it does not wash or scrub off.

You might notice

  • A mole that has changed in size, shape or colour, or one that bleeds or itches.
  • Small soft tags on the neck, underarms or eyelids that catch on chains or clothes.
  • A raised, thick or sunken scar after a cut, burn, surgery or pimples.
  • Red, purple or pale lines on the stomach, thighs, arms or back.

Why it happens.

01

Pigment cells and rubbing

Moles form when pigment cells grow together in one spot, and many appear in childhood or early adult life. Skin tags are common where skin rubs on skin, and are seen more with weight gain, pregnancy and diabetes.

02

How your skin heals

After a deep injury, skin repairs itself with scar tissue. Some people form thick raised scars called keloids, often on the chest, shoulders and ears.

03

Fast stretching of skin

Stretch marks appear when skin stretches quickly, as in pregnancy, growth spurts, weight gain or bodybuilding. Long use of strong steroid creams can also cause them.

At the clinic

What gets checked first.

The dermatologist looks at the mole closely, often with a handheld magnifier called a dermatoscope, and asks if it has changed. If a mole looks unusual, it may be removed and sent to a lab for testing. For scars and tattoos, the doctor checks the type, depth and colour, and asks if you have had thick raised scars before.

What treatment can involve

Mole removal as a minor procedure

What it isThe mole or skin tag is removed after the skin is numbed, and a mole may be sent to a lab for testing.

What to expectAny removal leaves some mark, usually a small flat scar that fades over months, and some moles can grow back.

Q-switched laser

What it isShort laser pulses break tattoo ink into smaller bits, which the body then clears slowly.

What to expectFading takes several sittings spaced six to eight weeks or more apart, black and dark blue inks respond better than colours like green and yellow, and a faint shadow or a lighter or darker patch can remain.

Microneedling with radiofrequency (MNRF)

What it isFine needles pass heat into the skin to help new collagen form under sunken scars and stretch marks.

What to expectScars can become shallower over several sittings and do not vanish, and stretch marks fade only partly.

Chemical peels

What it isA mild acid is applied to the skin to remove the top layer, which can help surface marks and dark marks left by pimples.

What to expectPeels need several sittings, help colour and shallow marks, and do little for deep scars.

Prescription medicines and creams

What it isThese include retinoid creams as prescribed for new stretch marks, and medicines for thick raised scars.

What to expectCreams give a small improvement, mostly on new red or purple stretch marks and little on old white ones, and retinoid creams are not used in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Straight talk

A mole or skin tag is usually removed in one visit, and the spot heals in about one to three weeks. Tattoo fading and scar treatment take many months, and the skin does not go back to exactly how it was. Skin tags can form again in new places, and keloids can return after treatment.

What helps at home

  • Do not cut, tie or burn moles and skin tags at home, and avoid acid or mole removal creams sold online.
  • Do not pick or scratch scabs, pimples or healing wounds.
  • Use sunscreen on new scars and treated skin, because sun makes marks darker.
  • Take a clear photo of any mole you are watching, so a change is easy to see.

When to come in soon

Do not wait if a mole changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds, itches, or looks different from your other moles. A new dark spot in adult life that keeps growing also needs a check.

Questions people ask.

Is my mole dangerous?

Most moles are harmless and stay the same for years. A mole that changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds, or looks unlike your other moles should be checked. A new dark spot on the palm, sole or under a nail also needs a look.

Will mole removal leave a scar?

Yes, any removal leaves some mark. It is usually small and flat, and it fades over months. Tell the doctor if you have had thick raised scars before.

How many sittings does tattoo removal take?

It depends on the ink colour, how deep the ink is and your skin. Most tattoos need several sittings, spaced six to eight weeks or more apart. Black and dark blue inks fade better than green or yellow, and some tattoos do not clear fully.

Can stretch marks be removed completely?

No. New red or purple marks respond better to treatment than old white ones. Treatment can make them less visible, and they do not go away fully.
