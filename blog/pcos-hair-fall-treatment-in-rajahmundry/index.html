What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects people with ovaries, primarily during their reproductive years. PCOS is characterized by a variety of symptoms, and its exact cause is not fully understood. The condition is associated with an imbalance of reproductive hormones, particularly elevated levels of androgens (male hormones)

Symptoms of PCOS-Related Hair Loss

Hair loss related to Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) can present in different ways. Here are some symptoms and characteristics associated with PCOS-related hair loss:

Thinning Hair:
Hair thinning is a common symptom. It may occur gradually, with an overall reduction in hair density.

Increased Hair Shedding:
Individuals with PCOS-related hair loss may notice an increase in daily hair shedding. This can be observed during washing, brushing, or throughout the day.

Male-Pattern Baldness (Androgenetic Alopecia):
PCOS can lead to an excess of androgens (male hormones) in the body, which may cause hair loss patterns similar to male-pattern baldness. This often involves hair thinning at the crown or a receding hairline.


Excess Body Hair (Hirsutism):
While not directly related to scalp hair loss, excess body hair (hirsutism) is a common symptom of PCOS. This may include increased hair growth on the face, chest, back, and other areas.
Scalp Sensitivity or Pain:

Some individuals with PCOS-related hair loss may experience sensitivity or pain on the scalp. This can be associated with inflammation or changes in the hair follicles.

Irregular Menstrual Cycles:
PCOS often leads to irregular menstrual cycles or an ovulation. Changes in the menstrual cycle may be an indication of hormonal imbalances contributing to hair loss.
